Wildlife rescue and rehabilitation in Malaysia

A second chance
for Malaysia’s wildlife

Every year, thousands of wild animals end up in human hands: found in gardens, hit on roads, seized from traders. What happens next? Follow public records from the people who take them in, nurse them back to health and return them to the forest.

01 · Into care

Thousands of animals are handed in every year

When someone finds a wild animal in trouble, or another agency confiscates one, it can be handed over to PERHILITAN, the Department of Wildlife and National Parks. Its annual reports count these handovers for each state in Peninsular Malaysia.

Snakes, turtles and monitor lizards, more than monkeys

Reptiles made up 14,146 of the animals handed over in 2024. Mammals made up 1,683 and birds just 205.

A handover is a record of contact between people and wildlife. It does not count how many animals live in the wild.

02 · Recovery

Recovery takes time, and not every story ends well

Rehabilitation means preparing an animal to survive on its own again. Guidelines for Malaysia’s national rescue centre describe a quarantine period on arrival, regular health and behaviour checks, and release only for animals that are healthy and behave like wild animals. International guidance adds that animals must not depend on people for food.

Source: PERHILITAN 2024, numeric table under Figure 26 (p107); 2023, Table 31 and text. The 2024 table is used where some of its bar labels differ.

A closer look · Sun bears in Sabah

Nine bears, five years of records

The Bornean Sun Bear Conservation Centre (BSBCC) in Sepilok cares for bears that were orphaned, kept as pets or confiscated. Bears that are ready are taken to Tabin Wildlife Reserve.

Some are released straight into the forest (a hard release). Others, like Tenom and Pitang, first spend months on supervised forest walks.

Sources: BSBCC Annual Reports 2021–2025; Brunton et al. 2025 for Sunbearo’s and Loki’s outcomes; background: NWRC sun-bear guideline (2022) and IUCN (2025). No animal tracking data is used.

03 · Return

Back to the forest

In 2024 the National Wildlife Rescue Centre returned 32 animals of 16 species to six protected areas. Where an animal goes depends on what it needs: big forests for big cats and bears, a coastal park for eagles and owls.

Reading the map

Penang National Park received the most

13 animals, mostly birds of prey, owls and small mammals, went to Penang National Park.

The largest animals went to the largest forests. Leopards went to Temenggor Forest Reserve, and clouded leopards and sun bears to Taman Negara in Terengganu.

Dots mark the approximate position of each protected area. Lines show where animals were sent, not the roads they travelled or exact release spots.

Source: PERHILITAN 2024, Table 31 (p108). Area positions: approximate locations. The map and the Sankey show the same 32 animals; do not add them together.

04 · People

Care also takes people’s time

At BSBCC, volunteers prepare food, feed bears, build enrichment toys and clean enclosures. University interns also run school talks. The centre lists every volunteer placement in its annual report.

Sources: BSBCC Annual Reports 2021–2025; 2025 placements from Appendix I (pp30–32). Placement length = end date − start date + 1. Names are not shown.

Care is more than a rescue count.

It is a chain of decisions, places and people.

Handovers are rising and the animals keep coming. Some recover and go home to the forest; others never can. Behind every number in these reports are keepers, vets and volunteers giving their time so wild animals get a second chance.

How to read these figures
  • Handovers are records of contact, not estimates of how many animals live in the wild or how well a state protects wildlife.
  • Handovers, rescue-centre intake, releases and the BSBCC bears are separate records. Dividing one by another does not give a success rate.
  • The bear timeline shows cases described in public reports, not every bear at BSBCC.
  • Volunteer placement days are calendar days on site, not hours worked, and do not measure the effect on animals.
  • Extraction notes and page references for every figure are in the data provenance record.
